# Introducing Finch

Finch is an alternative keyboard layout inspired by Canary. It uses a thumb alpha and (optional) adaptive swaps. Its primary focus is extremely low pinky usage, high rolls and not compromising on the standard stats for alternative keyboard layouts. 

<br>
The inspiration for Finch came from my previous use of Colemak and Aptmak. Colemak was my alternative layout and Aptmak brought me to using E on my thumb. However eventually with both I ended up with pinky pain with the load from letters like A, O, and R. Canary interested me due to the high rolls, good flow low pinky usage on the left. 

<br>
Pinky usage is really what separates Finch from other layouts, it's not for everyone but if you want to reduce pinky load it does that very well. Other attempts such as BEAKL succeeded at pinky usage reduction but the rest of the layout suffers greatly.

# Statistics

Despite the limitations of pinky usage Finch comes out in the top 98th percentile for rolls, bottom 0th percentile for SFB, only 0.08% bad redirects, better than average stretch and scissors, but with high index finger usage and not the greatest SFS though the SFS are primarily on the indexes.

# Intended Use
Finch was designed with a low keycount hummingbird keyboard such as my 26 key [ʻākohekohe](https://github.com/grassfedreeve/akohekohe/) in mind. However it does still work with larger 30 key hummingbirds or column stagger keyboards. If you prefer rowstagger there is a slightly tweaked nokwts version of finch that I have added at the bottom of this page.

<br>
If you are using a larger board, V is placed above C to approximate the statistics as combos are not considered in layout analysers. You can absolutely move V off of the left pinky if desired or use the V combo on larger boards, even with keys I personally believe combo V is more comfortable than corner placement.

<br>
You can see my personal keymap with all the combos, layers and fun used to compress a full keyboard down to 25% of the keys on [KeymapDB](https://keymapdb.com/keymaps/grassfedreeve/).

<br>
# Combos

This low keycount intends to have V, QU, Z, and J combos instead of dedicated keys. Z and J are infrequent and don't need much explanation. Q is also infrequent and almost always followed by U so a QU combo is recommend. Adaptive Magic could be used here as well to add a u after q to most letters but I have never bothered to take the time for that.

<br>
V is a more common key and may need more explanation. V is an almost a special case that works really well as a combo. V almost exclusively interacts with vowels, especially E and I. So with optimised layouts, it gets pushed to one of the worse positions on the consonant side. Usually top pinky or top inner. 

<br>
The combo position recommended is easier to reach and use over the pinkie or inner index. It is predictably preceded and followed by a vowel (or space), it is easy to keep a typing flow with the combo. 

<br>
<sup>This explanation for V is stolen and reworded from [jcmkk3](https://github.com/jcmkk3/bird-layout).</sup>

### Positions

- `l+y=z`
- `yw=v`
- `gx=j`
- `gd=qu`

<br>
Combo positions have been optimised using akl.gg, but can be adjusted if preferred. For British and Australian English I recommend swapping the J and Z combos as Z is rarely used so the potential scissors can be ignored.

<br>
# Punctuation

Something you will find with modern AKL layouts is the frequent use of ao' and a ,i. pinky column, which are fairly optimal punctuation options for english. However when optimising for a single pinky key the pinky punctuation is no longer an option. By putting E on the right thumb we can keep the ,i. column and put a low frequency vowel U on pinky keeping usage within 3% without sacrificing punctuation.

<br>
# Thumb Rolls and Redirects

A good chunk of the rolls in Finch's stats are on the thumbs with space and E, some people do not count or consider thumb rolls as "real." I would argue these are real and can easily be seen by rapidly typing space+THE with finch. With left thumb space and right thumb E you can see a nice roll pattern throughout the movement. Swapping thumbs and trying again you can see an uncomfortable redirect instead. 

<br>
# Adaptive Magic

You can always find the up to date magic rules on [akl.gg](https://akl.gg/#.gQC6MDFNMkNaQUdQTkVTSENEV0VTNUVFVkNCMFg). 

<br>
Finch uses adaptive swaps in order to improve comfort. Adaptive swaps swap the position of two keys depending on the key that preceded it. For example system can be quite slow to type with an sy column, to remove this after s is pressed r and y swap places. This way you type s**r**stem to output system. This allows the more common sequences to become comfortable rolls, and the uncommon sequences the SFBs. 

<br>
Unlike the rest of the layout I think adaptives may need tweaking and I **encourage** tweaking them. Personally due to the yl bigram being very common at my work I have that swap disabled. You may similarly find that you type a word frequently that has a very uncommon bigram. The eu → ee magic is great for removing those repeats but was a bit much on my very injured pinkies.

<br>
### Repeats

- `l w↔l`
- `c x↔c`
- `f w↔f`
- `p w↔p`
- `e u↔e`

### Swaps

- `g x↔s`
- `g d↔y`
- `r h↔l`
- `s r↔y`
- `t g↔w`
- `u h↔'`
- `y l↔s`

<br>
If you don't want to bother with adaptives, it is optional. I used finch without any adaptives for a year without any issue. Adaptives here just fix the small pain points such as rl and sy for a more comfortable experience. Or they add new rolls to replace repeats like lw → ll. However, Finch has perfectly fine statistics without them, with only 0.438% SFB and is very comfortable.

# Row Stagger
If you prefer rowstagger I will note that finch was not designed with rowstagger in mind but it is still perfectly usable if you have a split spacebar or use widemod to use right alt for E. This version does take slight advantage of nokwts fingering for comfort and less adaptive magic.
